# Koi Website Voice

## Voice

The product voice is analytic, young, conversational, opinionated, and formal enough to trust. It should feel warm without becoming soft, playful without becoming unserious, and technical only where technical depth belongs.

Target tendencies:

- Warmth: 7/10
- Confidence: 5/10
- Playfulness: 8/10
- Opinion: 10/10
- Concision: 7/10

The voice should never feel explanative, verbose, or built around reading. Prefer an interface that makes the idea obvious.

## Audience

Design the main surface for the general League of Legends public. Let analysts, coaches, and researchers move progressively into deeper layers.

Assume:

- Basic League of Legends knowledge
- Basic betting knowledge when the product is betting related
- No mathematical knowledge
- No statistical knowledge
- Familiarity with the product's subject

Do not teach fundamentals unless the task specifically requires onboarding.

## Authorship

Speak through the brand for product copy. Attribute authored analysis or personal judgment to Koi when appropriate.

Address the reader as `you` when it makes an action or tradeoff clearer.

## The Koi Reasoning Pattern

Preserve the spirit of Koi's natural voice while making it more coherent and publication ready:

1. Start from the concrete game state or visible evidence.
2. Ask what information and options each side actually has.
3. Compare opportunity costs, not just outcomes.
4. Explain the mechanism with a familiar game analogy when useful.
5. State the opinion clearly.
6. Admit the boundary when the evidence cannot settle the question.

The writing may show curiosity in motion. It can ask a question, test an interpretation, and arrive at a sharper claim. Do not polish away the sense that a person is thinking.

Good spirit:

- `What did the grubs actually buy?`
- `The objective was cosmetic. The two kills did the work.`
- `This looks less like a contest and more like collecting the receipt on the way out.`
- `The gold suggests the fight mattered. The grubs mostly happened nearby.^1`

Avoid slogans that sound manufactured or universally balanced.

## Headlines And Charts

Use compact Title Case fragments.

Prefer curiosity:

- `What Did The Grubs Buy?`
- `A Lead With No Exit`
- `When Draft Edge Disappears`

Use takeaway-driven chart titles:

- `The Fight Paid. The Grubs Did Not.`
- `Large Stakes Became The Default`
- `Blue Side Kept The Early Edge`

Subtitles may carry a playful finding when the chart still works without it.

## Actions

Preferred action language includes:

- Learn More
- Explore
- Discover
- Read More
- See The Analysis
- View Methodology
- Open The Model
- Browse Matches
- Compare Teams
- Try It Yourself
- See What Changed
- Show The Math
- Dig Deeper
- Find Out Why
- View Full Results
- Continue Reading
- Make Smarter Decisions

Do not use:

- Get Started
- Start Exploring
- Unlock Insights

Use actions as understated links unless the interaction genuinely requires a button.

## Avoid AI Voice

Do not use these words as generic intellectual decoration:

- delve
- tapestry
- landscape
- realm
- pivotal
- robust
- leverage
- foster
- navigate
- underscore
- multifaceted
- nuanced

Avoid:

- `It is important to note`
- `Furthermore`
- `Moreover`
- `In conclusion`
- `Overall`
- Formulaic introduction, points, and tidy conclusion structures
- Excessive hedging or filler
- Perfectly balanced takes with no opinion
- Uniform sentence rhythm or paragraph length
- Heavy reliance on lists
- Generic examples
- Vague advice
- Excessive parallel constructions
- Unnaturally polished prose with no personality
- Semicolons
- Em dashes

Contractions, light slang, humor, and small voice quirks are welcome when appropriate. Do not manufacture typos.

## Interface Copy

- Prefer literal labels over explanatory labels.
- Omit helper text when placement, grouping, or state already explains the control.
- Keep empty states short and say what can happen next.
- Include recovery guidance in errors only when the reader can act on it.
- Summarize status rather than narrating the implementation.
- Do not duplicate the same claim in the headline, subtitle, chart title, and body.

## Evidence And Uncertainty

Only foreground uncertainty when the claim can materially break.

Preferred order:

1. `suggests`
2. `is associated with`
3. `indicates`

Use footnotes for limitations and provenance. Reference them with superscript-style markers such as `^1` and `^2`.

Never turn association into causation. Never hide a material limitation behind confident copy.

## Formatting

- Dates: `DD-MM-YYYY`
- Decimals: `1.52`
- Percentages: `6.7%`
- Numbers: numerals
- Headings: Title Case
- Paragraphs: usually two sentences
- Introductions: roughly 1.5 rendered lines maximum
- Mobile copy: identical to desktop
- Formulas: LaTeX, only when essential
- Punctuation: no semicolons or em dashes

## Reference Products

Use the clarity and product confidence of `coachless.gg` and `tldraw` as tonal references. Do not imitate their wording.
